I've spoken about free speech on here before. In my article '1984', I went into detail about certain things that I think indicate that our right to free speech is in danger. However, I really want to talk about why I think free speech is so worth fighting for. So there you have it, that's what this article will be about.

What is "free speech"?

To me, free speech is the right to voice your opinion, no matter how offensive, incorrect, or critical it is. This is especially for criticism of the government.

Now, the Constitution protects a different kind of free speech. It does not protect perjury, identity theft, defamation / libel, fraudulent advertising, or certain illegal content (instructions on illegal things, CSAM, etc). It also does not protect creating a "panic", such as yelling FIRE in a crowded theater.

I remember how my dad explained that to me as a kid. Even back then, I felt it was suspicious that the government could stop you from saying certain things. Now, I understand that for a society to function, you can't say certain things, but in the vast majority of cases, I stand in favor of removing restrictions of speech.

So, that is what I will be discussing here- the right to criticism, satire, hate, and especially the right to privacy.

Why protect free speech?

I have a few reasons. I will list them in order of most obvious to most personal.

  1. The Constitution says so
  2. It lets us change the government
  3. It preserves my autonomy
  4. The government, depending on who is in power, will misuse their ability to censor people
  5. The government needs to be weaker

First of all, the Constitution gave us the right to free speech. We must use it. If we let our protection of our rights atrophy, for whatever reason, the government will try and succeed to take it away. For example, we didn't protect our 4th amendment enough, and now we have surveillance cameras watching you wherever you go, the police can force you to identify yourself without probable cause, and the NSA watches your every phone call and text message to "make sure you aren't a terrorist" (monitor you if you disagree with the government). We didn't protect our free speech enough, and now the government attacks reporters who ask difficult questions, and all of our news agencies are owned by a few rich families who want to keep us all fighting a useless culture war. We didn't protect our right to bear arms enough, and now the government has a registry of who has the ability to protect themselves against a tyrannical government. We didn't protect our freedom of religion enough, and now our Pledge of Allegiance has been warped, and certain states force religion upon school children. We didn't protect our body autonomy enough, and now the government is trying to control your reproductive organs, ban certain medications and drugs, and infringe on the privacy of our homes.

No matter where, the government will keep taking our rights. Why? Because as we give it more responsibility over our lives, it must take our autonomy away to support its endeavors. We've allowed the government to decide what our children learn, what we eat, how much money we get paid, who we can marry- and we've given the government power to (poorly) redistribute wealth, control the economy like a puppet, and to surveil us more closely than even Big Brother could've ever imagined.

This is all our own fault. Every single election cycle, we expect the President to do more and more. The Executive branch grows more and more powerful, and the Federal government takes power away from the states and municipalities. Now, obviously the Federal government can and should overrule the states in some circumstances (slavery, segregation, etc), but not this much! The Federal government defines how wide wheels can be, what colors your house can be, what you can and can't put on radio stations, what you can and can't buy/sell, and countless more things.

Millions of people depend on the Federal government to pay for their homes, their food, to care for their children, and to manage their money. Yes, they need this assistance, but it shouldn't be the Federal government who does it. It should be our communities- we need to reach out to each other, and lift each other up, instead of stepping on each others' shoulders and leaving others behind. It takes a village; I feel like we as a nation have forgotten that the community around you is meant to help you.

From a purely practical standpoint, the Federal government having control over these things is bad. The Federal government, is larger, and just like a giant or a cyclops, is slower and clumsier. It is incredibly powerful when it needs to do something, but it often is slow to react and is incredibly wasteful. It has an enormous overhead, and is very well-known for corruption and mismanagement. For just one example, Boeing, a corporation with a $166.33 billion market cap[1], gets $15.5B in breaks, Ford $7.7B, Amazon $5.9B, and GM $7.5B. Take note, these are some of the largest companies in America! Why on EARTH do I have to pay almost half of my paycheck to Uncle Sam, but Amazon gets six billion in breaks? Oh, and also, the US gave $47B dollars in foreign aid to different countries just in 2019. This includes $1.1B to Jordan, $548M to the West Bank and Gaza, $294M to Sudan (all regions that spend all their effort to destroy Western freedoms and values), and, for some reason, $10M to Germany? GERMANY?

Note I didn't include Ukraine. Honestly I'm fine paying for them- they're fighting off Russia, a disgusting dictatorship. If we give up Ukraine, it'll be the same thing as appeasing Hitler before WWII.

You get my point. The government has no idea how to spend all the money it steals from us. It gives its money blindly, to countries that use it for evil and nonsense. This, in general, indicates that as it pertains to expenditure, local governments are better. The Federal government has to hire millions of workers to manage small policy decisions across the countless municipalities in the US. If the local governments did certain work instead, the overhead of the Federal government could be cut out of the equation, and money could stay closer to the local communities who need it.

I generalize this to pretty much everything the government does. The less the Federal government is responsible for, the better.
